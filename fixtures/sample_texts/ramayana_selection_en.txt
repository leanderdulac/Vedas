Ramayana of Valmiki — selected public-domain English narrative themes (study corpus)

BALA KANDA themes
In the land of Kosala, in the city of Ayodhya, ruled the righteous king Dasharatha of the Ikshvaku line. Desiring sons, he performed the ashvamedha and putriyeshti. From the sacrificial offering, queens Kausalya, Kaikeyi and Sumitra received divine payasa.
Kausalya bore Rama, the embodiment of dharma; Kaikeyi bore Bharata; Sumitra bore Lakshmana and Shatrughna. Rama grew beloved of all, skilled in arms and gentle in speech, the refuge of the good.

AYODHYA KANDA themes
Dasharatha resolved to anoint Rama as heir. Kaikeyi, influenced by Manthara, claimed two boons formerly granted: exile of Rama for fourteen years, and kingship for Bharata.
Rama accepted exile without anger, saying a promise of the father must not be broken, and dharma is higher than pleasure of the throne. Sita insisted on accompanying him; Lakshmana followed as devoted brother and guardian.
Bharata refused the throne as spoils of intrigue, placed Rama's sandals on the royal seat, and ruled as regent from Nandigrama, awaiting Rama's return.

ARANYA KANDA themes
In the forest, Rama protected sages from rakshasas. Shurpanakha approached and was repulsed; her brothers Khara and Dushana attacked and were defeated.
Ravana, hearing of Sita's beauty, planned abduction. Maricha as a golden deer drew Rama away; Lakshmana left Sita under compulsion of her fear for Rama. Ravana in the guise of an ascetic seized Sita and carried her to Lanka.
Jatayu fought Ravana and fell wounded, telling Rama of the southern flight of the demon king.

KISHKINDHA KANDA themes
Rama allied with Sugriva, wronged by Vali. Rama slew Vali in combat according to the alliance's terms, and Sugriva was crowned. The search for Sita was organized across the quarters; Hanuman, son of the Wind, led the southern party.

SUNDARA KANDA themes
Hanuman crossed the ocean, entered Lanka, found Sita in the ashoka grove, and gave her Rama's ring as token. He burned parts of Lanka and returned with news that Sita lived, devoted and unyielding, awaiting rescue.

YUDDHA KANDA themes
Rama's army crossed to Lanka. War followed between vanaras and rakshasas. Ravana fell in battle to Rama. Vibhishana was crowned. Sita underwent the ordeal of fire to affirm purity before the world. Rama returned to Ayodhya; the reign of Rama-rajya is described as an age of dharma, prosperity and protection of the people.

Themes for study: maryada-purushottama (Rama as ideal of righteous conduct), pativrata-dharma (Sita), fraternal loyalty (Lakshmana, Bharata), sevakabhakti (Hanuman), and the restoration of dharma against adharma embodied in Ravana's arrogance and violence.
