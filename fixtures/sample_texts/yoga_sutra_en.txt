Yoga-sutras of Patanjali — public-domain English rendering (study corpus)

SAMADHI PADA
1.1 Now begins the exposition of Yoga.
1.2 Yoga is the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind.
1.3 Then the Seer abides in its own true nature.
1.4 At other times the Seer appears to take the form of the mental fluctuations.
1.5 The mental modifications are fivefold; they are painful or not-painful.
1.6 Right knowledge, misconception, verbal delusion, sleep and memory.
1.12 Their restraint is by practice and non-attachment.
1.13 Practice is the effort to be firmly established in that state.
1.14 It becomes firmly grounded when carefully attended to for a long time without interruption and with devotion.
1.15 Non-attachment is the consciousness of mastery in one who is free from thirst for objects seen or heard.
1.16 The highest is absence of thirst for the gunas, due to knowledge of the Purusha.
1.20 Faith, energy, memory, samadhi and discernment follow for others.
1.23 Or by devotion to Ishvara.
1.24 Ishvara is a special Purusha, untouched by afflictions, actions, fruits of actions or residual impressions.
1.27 His expression is the sacred syllable OM (pranava).
1.28 Its repetition and meditation on its meaning.
1.33 By cultivation of friendliness toward the happy, compassion for the suffering, delight in the virtuous, and equanimity toward the non-virtuous, the mind becomes pure.

SADHANA PADA
2.1 Austerity, self-study and devotion to Ishvara constitute Kriya-Yoga.
2.3 The afflictions are ignorance, I-am-ness, attachment, aversion and clinging to life.
2.5 Ignorance is regarding the non-eternal as eternal, the impure as pure, pain as pleasure, and non-Self as Self.
2.16 What is to be escaped is pain yet to come.
2.28 By the practice of the limbs of Yoga, impurity is destroyed and the light of knowledge arises, leading to discriminative discernment.
2.29 Yama, niyama, asana, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana and samadhi are the eight limbs.
2.30 Non-injury, truthfulness, non-stealing, continence and non-possessiveness are the yamas.
2.32 Cleanliness, contentment, austerity, self-study and devotion to Ishvara are the niyamas.
2.46 Asana is steady and comfortable.
2.49 Pranayama is the regulation of the incoming and outgoing breath after asana is mastered.
2.54 Pratyahara is the withdrawal of the senses from their objects, as if imitating the nature of the mind.

VIBHUTI PADA
3.1 Concentration (dharana) is binding the mind to one place.
3.2 The unbroken flow of that is meditation (dhyana).
3.3 When only the object shines forth, as if empty of its own form, that is samadhi.
3.4 The three together are samyama.
3.5 By mastery of samyama comes the light of wisdom.

KAIVALYA PADA
4.34 Kaivalya is the establishment of the power of consciousness in its own nature when the gunas, devoid of purpose for the Purusha, resolve into their cause—or the power of consciousness abides in its own form.
