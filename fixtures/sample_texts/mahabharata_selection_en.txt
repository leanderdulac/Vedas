Mahabharata — selected public-domain English themes (study corpus)

ORIGIN AND KURU LINE
The Mahabharata narrates the conflict of the Bharata line, centered on the Kauravas and Pandavas. Bhishma, son of Shantanu and Ganga, took a terrible vow of celibacy for his father's happiness. Dhritarashtra, blind, and Pandu were of the next generation. The Pandavas—Yudhishthira, Bhima, Arjuna, Nakula and Sahadeva—were raised with the Kauravas headed by Duryodhana.

DICE AND EXILE
Yudhishthira, devoted to dharma yet drawn into a rigged game of dice, lost kingdom, wealth, brothers and Draupadi. Draupadi was insulted in the assembly; Bhima vowed terrible vengeance. The Pandavas went to forest exile for twelve years and one year of concealment.
Dharma questions fill the forest books: what is right action under oppression? How does a king protect without cruelty? What is the duty of a wife, a brother, a warrior?

BHAGAVAD-GITA SETTING
On Kurukshetra, armies arrayed, Arjuna despaired at fighting teachers and kin. Krishna, his charioteer and the Supreme Person in the text's theology, taught the Gita: the immortality of the Self, the discipline of action without attachment to fruits, devotion, and knowledge.

WAR AND AFTERMATH
The eighteen-day war destroyed nearly all warriors. Bhishma fell on a bed of arrows; Drona, Karna, and Duryodhana met their ends under complex conditions of dharma and strategy. After victory, Yudhishthira was heavy with grief; the Shanti and Anushasana parvans teach rajadharma, charity, ahimsa debates, and the paths of liberation.
The horse-sacrifice and later journey of the Pandavas toward the Himalayas close the epic cycle. Yudhishthira's trial of the dog and the vision of heaven and hell teach that dharma is subtle and that compassion is not separate from truth.

KEY IDEAS FOR INFERENCE
- Dharma is multi-layered: personal duty (svadharma), royal duty, and universal good sometimes conflict.
- The epic does not reduce ethics to a single slogan; it stages arguments.
- Krishna's teaching to Arjuna centers non-attachment, knowledge of the Self, and devotion.
- Draupadi's questions in the assembly challenge empty legalism without justice.
- The cost of adharma is civilizational, not merely personal.

SHANTI PARVA themes (selected)
A king should protect subjects as a pregnant woman protects the child in the womb. Force without dharma destroys; dharma without firmness is sterile. Truth is the highest dharma, yet truth must be held with wisdom, for speech that harms the innocent is not pure dharma.
Charity, self-restraint and compassion are ornaments of the powerful. The wise see the Self in all beings and do not delight in cruelty.
