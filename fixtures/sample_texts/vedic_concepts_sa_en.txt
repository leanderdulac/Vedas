Vedic conceptual lexicon (bilingual study notes — public-domain pedagogical fixture)

ॐ / OM / AUM
The sacred syllable. Mandukya: all time and the beyond is Aum. Yoga-sutra: pranava is the expression of Ishvara. Used as invocation, meditation support, and seal of peace (shanti).

धर्म / DHARMA
That which upholds. Cosmic order, social duty, righteousness, intrinsic nature of a being. In the Gita, decline of dharma occasions divine manifestation. In Ramayana, Rama upholds father's word as dharma even at personal cost. In Mahabharata, dharma is subtle (sukshma).

कर्म / KARMA
Action and its continuity of result. Gita: you have a right to action, not to fruits. Actions bind when done with attachment; sacrifice-oriented and knowledge-fire purify action. Brihadaranyaka: as one acts, so one becomes.

आत्मन् / ĀTMAN
The Self. Katha: unborn, eternal, not slain with the body. Chandogya: tat tvam asi — That thou art. Isha: see all beings in the Self and the Self in all beings.

ब्रह्मन् / BRAHMAN
The Absolute / ultimate reality. Mundaka: from the Imperishable beings issue and return. Taittiriya: from the Self space arose, cascading to food and person. Neti neti indicates Brahman by negation of limited attributes.

योग / YOGA
Union / discipline. Gita: evenness of mind is yoga; yoga is skill in action. Patanjali: yoga is stilling of mental fluctuations; eight limbs; kaivalya as establishment in pure consciousness.

भक्ति / BHAKTI
Devotion / loving participation. Gita ch.9 and 12: offering of leaf, flower, fruit, water with devotion; worship of the personal Lord and also of the unmanifest path.

ज्ञान / JNANA
Knowledge / liberating insight. Mundaka's higher knowledge (para vidya) apprehends the Imperishable. Gita: fire of knowledge burns actions to ash.

यज्ञ / YAJNA
Sacrifice / sacred offering. Rigvedic Agni as priest of sacrifice. Gita: world bound by actions other than those for sacrifice; perform action as yajna.

ऋत / RITA (ṚTA)
Vedic cosmic order and truth-rhythm preceding later dharma vocabulary. Agni is guardian of eternal Law in RV I.1 themes.

माया / MAYA
Measuring power / appearance. In later Vedanta, the power by which the One appears as many; related debates appear across Upanishadic and Gita layers (unmanifest form pervading all).

संसार / SAMSARA
The cycle of becoming. Katha and Gita address passage of the embodied through bodies; liberation is release from binding identification.

मोक्ष / MOKSHA / मुक्ति
Liberation. End of grief and knots of the heart (Mundaka). Gita 18: abandon all dharmas and take refuge — a theological climax of sharanagati.

पुरुष / PURUSHA
Person / conscious principle. Purusha Sukta: cosmic Person. Gita 15: perishable, imperishable, and the Supreme Person (Purushottama). Samkhya-Yoga: Purusha as seer distinct from prakriti.

प्रकृति / PRAKRITI
Nature / productive matrix. Gita 7: eightfold lower nature and higher life-nature. Yoga: gunas resolve when purpose for Purusha ends.

गुण / GUNA
Strands of nature — sattva, rajas, tamas (developed in Gita and Samkhya). Color experience, action and inertia.

इश्वर / ISHVARA
Lord. Yoga-sutra: special Purusha untouched by kleshas; devotion to Ishvara is a path. Theistic Upanishads (Shvetashvatara) and Gita center the personal Lord.

शान्ति / SHANTI
Peace. Closing formula: Om shanti shanti shanti — peace at levels of self, environment and divine forces.

These notes are pedagogical condensations for retrieval-augmented study; always verify against primary passages in the corpus.
